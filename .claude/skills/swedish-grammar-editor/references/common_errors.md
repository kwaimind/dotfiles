# Common Swedish Grammar Error Patterns

## Verb Conjugations

### få vs för
- **få** = verb "to get/receive/be allowed to"
  - Examples: får, fick, fått
  - "Jag får gå hem" (I may/can go home)
- **för** = preposition "for/because"
  - "Det är för sent" (It's too late)
  - "Jag gör det för dig" (I do it for you)

### vara vs vara
- **vara** = verb "to be"
  - Examples: är, var, varit
  - "Det kan vara svårt" (It can be difficult)
- **vare** = subjunctive form (rare in modern Swedish)

### de vs dem vs dom
- **de** = they (subject)
  - "De kommer imorgon" (They are coming tomorrow)
- **dem** = them (object)
  - "Jag ser dem" (I see them)
- **dom** = colloquial form (acceptable in informal writing)

### sen vs sedan
- **sen** = adjective "late" OR colloquial "then"
  - "Jag är sen" (I am late)
- **sedan** = formal "then/since"
  - "Sedan dess" (Since then)

## Articles (en/ett)

### en-words (common gender)
Most nouns use "en":
- en hund, en stol, en dag, en bok

### ett-words (neuter gender)
Roughly 25% of nouns:
- ett hus, ett barn, ett äpple, ett bord

### Patterns for ett-words
- Diminutives often use "ett"
- Places/locations sometimes use "ett"
- Abstract concepts vary
- **No reliable rule - must be memorized**

### Definite forms
- en-words: -en suffix
  - "en hund" → "hunden"
- ett-words: -et suffix
  - "ett hus" → "huset"

## Prepositions

### på vs i
- **på** = on (surface) OR at (activities/places)
  - "på bordet" (on the table)
  - "på jobbet" (at work)
  - "på svenska" (in Swedish - language)
- **i** = in (inside something)
  - "i huset" (in the house)
  - "i Sverige" (in Sweden - country)

### till vs åt
- **till** = to (direction/recipient)
  - "Jag går till affären" (I go to the store)
  - "ett brev till dig" (a letter to you)
- **åt** = for (benefit of someone)
  - "Jag lagar mat åt dig" (I cook food for you)

### från vs ifrån
- **från** = from (standard)
  - "Jag kommer från Stockholm"
- **ifrån** = from (more colloquial, emphasis on movement away)
  - Both are acceptable, "från" is more formal

### över vs ovanför
- **över** = over/across (can touch)
  - "bron över floden" (the bridge over the river)
- **ovanför** = above (doesn't touch)
  - "molnen ovanför staden" (clouds above the city)

## Word Order

### V2 Rule (Verb Second Position)
Main clauses: verb must be in second position
- "Igår åkte jag till Stockholm" (Yesterday went I to Stockholm)
- NOT "Igår jag åkte till Stockholm"

### Subordinate Clauses
Subject before verb in subordinate clauses:
- "Jag vet att hon kommer imorgon" (I know that she comes tomorrow)

### Adverbs
- Placement varies by clause type
- Main clause: after verb
  - "Jag äter ofta lunch klockan tolv"
- Subordinate clause: before verb
  - "...när jag ofta äter lunch"
